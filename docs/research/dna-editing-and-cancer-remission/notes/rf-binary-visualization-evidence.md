# RF, Electromagnetic Fields, DNA-as-Signal Claims and Visualization Practices in Cancer: Evidence Map (as of October 2026)

Scope note: this tests the premise that a "digital twin" holding a person's DNA as binary code, connected to RF and an AI visualizer, could act on the body or help remission. The evidence falls into four separate buckets: (1) physical electric fields applied to tissue through contact arrays (approved, modest benefit); (2) RF used as a heat source (ablation, hyperthermia); (3) the idea that information or signals that encode DNA can change biology (no supporting evidence); (4) mind-body and visualization interventions (quality-of-life benefit; survival evidence weak and contested). This is not medical advice.

## 1. Tumor Treating Fields (TTFields / Optune, Novocure): mechanism, approvals, survival figures, and why they are not data transmission

### Takeaway
TTFields are a real, FDA-approved electromagnetic cancer therapy with randomized survival benefit of roughly 2–5 months median in several cancers. They work only because insulated arrays taped to the skin over the tumor drive a low-intensity (1–3 V/cm) alternating electric field at a tumor-tuned frequency (about 150–200 kHz) through the tissue for many hours a day. No information content is involved: the effect depends on field strength, frequency, geometry and wear time, not on any encoded message.

### Cited Findings
- Mechanism: low-intensity (1–3 V/cm), intermediate-frequency alternating fields, with 200 kHz found optimal for GBM cells based on cell size and dielectric properties. In metaphase the field acts on the dipole moment of α/β-tubulin and disrupts spindle assembly. In late anaphase/telophase the non-uniform field at the cleavage furrow moves polar constituents such as septins by dielectrophoresis, which causes aberrant mitotic exit, aneuploidy and cell death. First characterized by Kirson et al. — [PMC review, TTFields and GBM microenvironment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13359516/); [Nature Sci Rep, mitotic spindle disruption](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep18046); [Kirson et al., alternating fields in animal models and human tumors](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/6285659_Alternating_electric_fields_arrest_cell_proliferation_in_animal_tumor_models_and_human_brain_tumors)
- Glioblastoma, EF-14 (Stupp et al., JAMA 2017;318(23):2306-2316): newly diagnosed GBM, TTFields plus maintenance temozolomide vs temozolomide alone; median OS 20.9 vs 16.0 months — [CURE](https://www.curetoday.com/view/tumor-treating-fields-improve-survival-in-glioblastoma); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT00916409](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00916409); [OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/tumor-treating-fields-substantially-improve-survival-in-gbm)
- In EF-14, higher device compliance (wear time) predicted better survival, which fits a dose-dependent physical mechanism — [J Neurooncol subgroup analysis](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11060-018-03057-z)
- Mesothelioma: NovoTTF-100L received FDA Humanitarian Device Exemption (H180002) on 23 May 2019. STELLAR was a single-arm phase 2 trial in 80 patients, median OS 18.2 months (95% CI 12.1–25.8) with chemotherapy. The comparison with about 12.1 months for chemotherapy is historical, not randomized — [Novocure SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1645113/000156459019020381/nvcr-ex991_14.htm); [ILCN commentary "More stars needed"](https://www.ilcn.org/ttfields-in-mesothelioma-the-stellar-trial-more-stars-needed-in-the-constellation/)
- NSCLC, LUNAR (phase 3, NCT02973789): FDA approved Optune Lua on 15 Oct 2024 for metastatic NSCLC after platinum, used concurrently with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors or docetaxel. Median OS was 13.2 vs 9.9 months overall; 18.5 vs 10.8 months in the ICI subgroup (n=66 TTFields arm); 11.1 vs 8.7 months in the docetaxel subgroup (n=71 TTFields arm). It was the first non-pharmacological therapy approved for metastatic NSCLC — [OncLive](https://www.onclive.com/view/fda-approves-optune-lua-for-metastatic-nsclc-after-platinum-based-chemotherapy); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/novocure-nets-fda-approval-tumor-treating-fields-metastatic-lung-cancer)
- Pancreatic, PANOVA-3 (phase 3, >500 patients, locally advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma, gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel ± TTFields 150 kHz): median OS 16.2 vs 14.2 months. Pain-free survival also improved. FDA approved Optune Pax on 11 Feb 2026, described as the first new treatment approach for locally advanced pancreatic cancer in about 30 years — [CancerNetwork](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/fda-approves-ttfields-plus-chemo-in-locally-advanced-pancreatic-cancer); [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/fda-approves-first-in-class-optune-pax-device-for-pancreatic-cancer); [Fierce Biotech](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/medtech/novocures-tumor-treating-electric-fields-clear-phase-3-pancreatic-cancer-trial); [PANOVA-3 protocol](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/91/NCT03377491/Prot_002.pdf)
- Delivery is "non-invasively through wearable arrays", meaning a body-worn, portable device — [CancerNetwork](https://www.cancernetwork.com/view/fda-approves-ttfields-plus-chemo-in-locally-advanced-pancreatic-cancer)

### Inferences
- TTFields are the strongest real-world proof that electromagnetic energy can treat cancer. They also show what that takes: a specific frequency band (100–300 kHz), field intensity of about 1–3 V/cm inside the tumor, contact electrodes placed by treatment planning, and roughly 18 or more hours a day of wear. A radio broadcast of a DNA file meets none of these conditions. RF from ordinary transmitters is GHz, not kHz. Far-field intensities at a body are many orders of magnitude lower. Encoding is irrelevant, because a sine field at 200 kHz carries no message.
- Effect sizes are modest (median OS gains of about 2–5 months) and always come as an add-on to chemotherapy or immunotherapy. None of the trials showed remission from fields alone.
- EF-14 and LUNAR were open-label with no sham device. Critics have raised this as a limitation. I did not fetch a primary critique in this session (see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Exact EF-14 hazard ratios and 5-year survival (13% vs 5% in the JAMA paper, from memory) were not verified from the primary source in this session.
- The mesothelioma comparator (12.1 months) comes from historical controls. I found no randomized mesothelioma TTFields trial result.
- I did not retrieve a primary critique on lack of sham control or blinding.

## 2. RF ablation and hyperthermia in oncology

### Takeaway
In oncology, RF is a tool for delivering heat. Radiofrequency ablation (RFA) passes current from a needle electrode to cook a small tumor to coagulative necrosis. Regional hyperthermia (about 40–43 °C, RF/microwave antennas) is used to sensitize tumors to chemotherapy or radiation. Both are evidence-based and both act through deposited thermal energy, not through information.

### Cited Findings
- RFA vs surgical resection for small hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC): in RCTs, 1- and 3-year OS did not differ, but 5-year OS and recurrence-free survival were lower with RFA. RFA had fewer complications and the same mortality — [PLoS One meta-analysis of RCTs and non-RCTs](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0084484)
- An updated meta-analysis of tumors ≤3 cm found that resection gives better survival and lower recurrence. For tumors ≤2 cm, 3-year OS was comparable — [Systematic Reviews 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13643-025-03057-x); [PMC 23-study meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11823996/)
- Regional hyperthermia, EORTC 62961-ESHO 95 (Issels et al., JAMA Oncology 2018): 341 patients with localized high-risk soft tissue sarcoma received neoadjuvant chemotherapy (EIA) ± regional hyperthermia. Local PFS HR 0.65 (95% CI 0.49–0.86; P=.002). 5-year OS 62.7% vs 51.3%. 9-year OS 54% vs 43% (median follow-up 74 months) — [JAMA Oncology](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaoncology/fullarticle/2672386); [EORTC](http://www.eortc.org/news/regional-hyperthermia-increases-benefit-of-chemotherapy-in-patients-with-high-risk-soft-tissue-sarcoma-in-ehso-eortc-trial-62961/); [Nature Rev Clin Oncol highlight](https://www.nature.com/articles/nrclinonc.2018.38)
- A systematic review of hyperthermia in high-risk STS is available — [Int J Hyperthermia 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02656736.2023.2236337)
- Mechanism: in tissue, RF energy acts on ions and polar molecules (mostly water) and becomes kinetic energy, then heat — [PMC, non-thermal effects review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7417565/); [Ovid, J Radiat Cancer Res](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/jrcr/fulltext/10.4103/jrcr.jrcr_17_22~non-ionizing-radiations-and-their-biochemical-and-biomedical)

### Inferences
- Therapeutic RF in oncology requires watts of power delivered into a small volume (ablation) or a controlled temperature rise (hyperthermia). The benefit scales with deposited energy and temperature. Signal content does not matter. RF that is weak enough to be safe for broadcast, which is the regime a "digital twin over radio" would use, deposits too little energy to have either effect.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve specific RFA guideline indications (BCLC early stage, renal, lung, bone metastases) or local control rates by organ.
- I did not retrieve hyperthermia + radiotherapy trials (e.g., cervical cancer, Dutch Deep Hyperthermia Trial) in this session.

## 3. RF exposure and cancer risk: IARC, WHO-commissioned reviews 2024–2025, ICNIRP limits

### Takeaway
IARC classified RF-EMF as Group 2B ("possibly carcinogenic") in 2011 on limited evidence of glioma in heavy mobile phone users. The 2024 WHO-commissioned review of human studies (Karipidis et al.) found moderate-certainty evidence of no increased brain cancer risk. A 2025 WHO-commissioned animal review (Mevissen et al.) found high-certainty evidence of increased glioma and heart schwannoma in male rats at high exposures. IARC lists RF for re-evaluation, no earlier than about late 2027. The debate is about possible harm at high, chronic exposure. No body of evidence suggests that RF exposure helps cancer.

### Cited Findings
- IARC 2011: 30 scientists from 14 countries classified RF-EMF as Group 2B, based on "limited evidence" from increased glioma risk among heavy mobile phone users (Lancet Oncology; Monograph 102) — [ScienceDaily/IARC release](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2011/05/110531133115.htm); [IARC Monograph 102, NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK304630/)
- IARC Advisory Group for 2025–2029 rated RF "high priority" for re-evaluation, advised for the latter half of the period (late 2027 at the earliest). A similar 2019 recommendation did not lead to a re-evaluation in 2020–2024 — [JEIC](https://www.jeic-emf.jp/english/academic/info/18246.html); [Microwave News](https://microwavenews.com/news-center/iarc-again-advised-review-rf-cancer-risk)
- Karipidis et al., Environment International 2024 (WHO-commissioned): screened more than 5,000 studies (1994–2022) and included 63. Moderate-certainty evidence that mobile phone use likely does not increase risk of glioma, meningioma, acoustic neuroma, pituitary or salivary gland tumours in adults, or paediatric brain tumours — [University of Wollongong](https://www.uow.edu.au/media/2024/mobile-phones-are-not-linked-to-brain-cancer.php); [ASCO Post](https://ascopost.com/news/september-2024/mobile-phones-may-not-be-linked-to-brain-cancer-who-backed-study-finds/); [Scimex](https://www.scimex.org/newsfeed/news-briefing-who-review-confirms-mobile-phones-not-linked-to-brain-cancer)
- A companion WHO-commissioned review of occupational and general-population exposure appeared in Environment International in 2025 — [ScienceDirect S016041202500025X](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016041202500025X)
- The ICBE-EMF advocacy group contests Karipidis (2024), saying critical methodological flaws make it unreliable — [ICBE-EMF fact sheet](https://icbe-emf.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/WHO-Research-Review-on-Cell-Phone-Radiation-and-Cancer-Wireless-Health-Effects-ICBE-EMF-Factsheet-1.pdf); [SaferEMR](https://www.saferemr.com/2024/09/biased-who-commissioned-review-claims.html)
- Mevissen et al., Environment International, April 2025 (WHO-commissioned): 52 animal studies including 20 chronic bioassays. High-certainty evidence of increased glioma and malignant heart schwannoma in male rats, and moderate certainty for several other neoplasms. Germany's BfS rejected the cancer finding, and a group led by Karipidis (ICNIRP vice chair) called the review flawed — [Microwave News](https://microwavenews.com/news-center/who-review-sees-rf-cancer-risk-animals); [ARPANSA commentary](https://www.arpansa.gov.au/commentary-systematic-review-radiofrequency-field-exposure-and-animal-cancer)
- ICNIRP 2020 basic restriction: whole-body average SAR of 0.08 W/kg for the general public from 100 kHz to 300 GHz, averaged over 30 min. A reduction factor of 50 is applied for the public — [ICNIRP 2020 guidelines PDF](https://www.icnirp.org/cms/upload/publications/ICNIRPrfgdl2020.pdf); [TEC India white paper](https://tec.gov.in/pdf/Studypaper/White%20Paper%20on%20Human%20Exposure%20Requirement%20for%20EMF%20Exposure%20in%20view%20of%20ICNIRP%202020%20Regulation.pdf)

### Inferences
- A fair reading: human epidemiology is reassuring (moderate certainty), the animal data at high whole-body exposures remain contested, and the regulatory classification is still 2B. Either way, the open question is whether RF causes cancer. No study proposes that it cures cancer. The only established therapeutic RF effects are thermal (section 2) or come from contact-delivered kHz fields (section 1).
- ICNIRP limits are set to prevent tissue heating. Any legal consumer RF transmitter used for a "digital twin" link would therefore stay far below the energy levels that have a therapeutic effect.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Microwave News pages beyond the snippets, so exposure levels in the rat bioassays (NTP, Ramazzini) are not given here.
- I found no update showing an IARC RF re-evaluation scheduled or completed as of October 2026.

## 4. Can a DNA sequence transmitted as binary or radio signals (or "DNA teleportation") affect cells or a person? Physics of RF photon energy

### Takeaway
No peer-reviewed, independently replicated evidence shows that a DNA sequence encoded as data, binary or radio waves affects living cells or a person. The best-known claim, Montagnier's "DNA waves" and teleportation work from 2009–2011, was published with weak peer review and no proper controls, and has never been independently replicated. It is widely classed as pseudoscience. Physics also rules it out: RF photons carry micro- to milli-electronvolts, compared with roughly 0.1–4 eV for biologically relevant bonds and about 27 meV of thermal noise at body temperature. A modulated signal also has no means of "addressing" a specific gene in a body.

### Cited Findings
- Montagnier et al. 2011, "DNA waves and water", Journal of Physics: Conference Series 306:012007. Highly diluted DNA (up to 10⁻¹⁰) was said to emit EM signals that were recorded as WAV files, sent electronically, replayed onto pure water for 1 hour, then PCR-amplified to give DNA "98% identical" to the original — [Wikipedia: DNA teleportation (summary with references)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_teleportation); [arXiv 1104.3113 preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.3113)
- Reception: earlier papers appeared in *Interdisciplinary Sciences: Computational Life Sciences*, whose editorial board Montagnier chaired. One was accepted within 3 days of submission. Philip Ball wrote that it "was never replicated". No plausible mechanism is known. The idea is likened to Benveniste's 1988 water memory — [Wikipedia: DNA teleportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_teleportation)
- Critics noted the arXiv paper reports no positive or negative controls. PCR is extremely sensitive to contamination, the obvious alternative explanation — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_teleportation); [ScienceBlogs "Respectful Insolence"](https://scienceblogs.com/insolence/2011/01/14/the-nobel-disease-meets-dna-teleportatio)
- Physics: RF is non-ionizing because its photon energy cannot ionize matter. Breaking bonds or ionizing needs several eV to keV, and microwaves fall far below that — [Wiley, Patty's Toxicology RF chapter](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/0471435139.tox101.pub2); [ScienceDirect topic overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/biochemistry-genetics-and-molecular-biology/radiofrequency-radiation)
- RF interacts with tissue mainly by moving ions and rotating polar water molecules, which produces heat — [PMC7417565](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7417565/)

### Inferences
- Worked numbers, computed from E = hf with h ≈ 4.136×10⁻¹⁵ eV·s (standard constant; my arithmetic):
  - 200 kHz (TTFields): about 8×10⁻¹⁰ eV
  - 2.4 GHz (Wi-Fi/Bluetooth): about 1×10⁻⁵ eV (10 µeV)
  - 60 GHz (mmWave): about 0.25 meV
  - thermal energy kT at 310 K: about 27 meV
  - covalent C–C bond: about 3.6 eV
  - So a 2.4 GHz photon is about 2,700× below kT and about 360,000× below a covalent bond. Single RF photons cannot cause targeted chemistry. The only routes are bulk heating or forces from macroscopic fields, and neither carries sequence-specific information.
- Information versus energy: a 3-billion-base genome encoded as bits and sent over radio reaches the body as a slightly varying field of microwatts per cm² or less. The body has no receptor that demodulates digital data. Cells "read" DNA through molecular machinery (polymerases, transcription factors) acting on physical molecules, not on electromagnetic representations of sequence. Encoding a DNA file in binary is a correct and useful data practice (storage, analysis, CRISPR guide design), but the file has no causal path back to the body without a physical intervention such as a drug, gene therapy or applied field.
- A testable version of the premise would be a double-blind, sham-controlled cell-culture experiment (personal DNA-encoded RF vs random-bit RF vs no RF at matched power, measuring proliferation and gene expression). Physics and the Montagnier record both predict a null result. Any reported effect would need contamination controls and independent replication.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study, positive or negative, that specifically tests "a person's own genome as binary over RF" on cells or on patients. The absence reflects the lack of any plausible mechanism. It is not an unexplored lead with promising pilot data.
- I did not retrieve formal replication attempts of Montagnier, beyond secondary sources reporting that none succeeded.

## 5. Guided imagery/visualization, meditation and psychological interventions: survival/remission vs quality of life

### Takeaway
Guided imagery and the Simonton method reliably help comfort, mood and anxiety, but there is no good evidence that they cause remission. The famous Spiegel 1989 survival result failed to replicate in two large RCTs (Goodwin 2001, n=235; Spiegel 2007, n=125). A 2026 preregistered meta-analysis co-authored by Spiegel (32 RCTs, 5,704 patients) reports a small pooled survival benefit (HR 0.80) across mixed psychosocial interventions. It is underpowered (mean power 17%), heterogeneous (I² 48%, prediction interval crossing 1), and isolates no imagery-specific effect. Earlier meta-analyses disagree.

### Cited Findings
- Simonton claims: Simonton, Matthews-Simonton & Sparks (1980) said guided imagery nearly doubled mean survival. The work has been criticized for lacking well-controlled studies — [ScienceDirect topic overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/immunology-and-microbiology/guided-imagery)
- CAM-Cancer: no controlled clinical trials of the Simonton Method exist, and one uncontrolled, flawed trial allows no conclusions on survival or QoL — [CAM-Cancer, Simonton Method](https://cam-cancer.org/simonton-method)
- Systematic review of guided imagery as an adjuvant cancer therapy (Roffe, Schmidt & Ernst, DARE-assessed): 6 RCTs with detailed results for 4. Pooling was impossible and quality was low on average. Imagery "may be psycho-supportive and increase comfort", with no compelling evidence for physical symptom effects — [NCBI Bookshelf DARE NBK71509](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK71509/)
- Some individual studies report immune changes, such as NK cell activity in a breast cancer study, but these surrogates have not been tied to survival — [ScienceDirect topic overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/immunology-and-microbiology/guided-imagery)
- Goodwin et al., NEJM 2001: 235 women with metastatic breast cancer, randomized 2:1 to supportive-expressive group therapy (n=158) or control (n=77). Median survival 17.9 vs 17.6 months, HR 1.06. Mood and pain perception improved, especially in more distressed women — [PubMed 11742045](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11742045/); [NEJM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa011871)
- Spiegel et al., Cancer 2007, Spiegel's own replication: 125 women (64 in weekly group therapy). Median survival 31 vs 33 months, with no overall survival effect — [Cancer (Wiley)](https://acsjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/cncr.22890); [Stanford news 2007](https://med.stanford.edu/news/all-news/2007/07/support-groups-dont-extend-survival-of-metastatic-breast-cancer-patients-stanford-study-finds.html)
- Kissane et al., Psycho-Oncology 2007: another supportive-expressive RCT in metastatic breast cancer, reporting survival and psychosocial outcomes. Only the title was retrieved, so I have not verified its survival result or figures — [PubMed 17385190](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17385190/)
- The ACS reported that group therapy fails to improve breast cancer survival — [ACS press release](https://pressroom.cancer.org/releases?item=50)
- Asakawa-Haas, Spiegel et al., *Communications Psychology* 2026: preregistered systematic review, meta-analysis and multiverse analysis of 32 RCTs (n=5,704). Survival HR 0.80 (95% CI 0.71–0.90). I² = 48%, 95% prediction interval HR 0.49–1.29. Median survival gain 3.9 months (95% CI −0.7 to 8.5; 16 trials). Risk of bias was "some concerns" in 30 trials and "high" in 2. Mean post hoc power 17% (6–40%). Certainty was rated moderate. No single component (education, CBT, expressive, group support) was clearly associated with stronger effects. Relaxation (k=8), mindfulness (k=4) and hypnosis (k=2) appeared as components but were not analysed separately. Funnel plot asymmetry was seen, while p-uniform and PET-PEESE did not confirm bias — [PMC13005039](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13005039/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00414-x)
- An earlier meta-analysis found that psychosocial interventions overall were not associated with better survival, though it reported benefit at 1 and 2 years — [Fu et al., Annals of Palliative Medicine](https://apm.amegroups.org/article/view/9641/html)
- A broader PLoS Medicine meta-analysis of 106 RCTs on psychosocial support and survival across healthcare settings, not cancer-specific — [PLoS Med 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosmedicine/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pmed.1003595)

### Inferences
- A fair summary: psychosocial care improves distress, mood and pain and is standard supportive care. The survival question is still open. The newest meta-analysis is positive but fragile (wide prediction interval, low power, co-authored by the field's main proponent). The best-powered single RCTs in metastatic breast cancer were null. Guided imagery or visualization specifically has no controlled survival evidence at all.
- If psychosocial interventions do extend survival, the proposed routes are behavioural and physiological: adherence to treatment, stress-hormone/HPA effects, sleep and social support. None of these routes involves encoding DNA or transmitting signals.

### Gaps
- I did not find or fetch a Cochrane review specific to guided imagery or meditation and cancer survival in this session.
- I did not retrieve Spiegel 1989 (Lancet) figures (originally reported as about 36.6 vs 18.9 months), so I list them only as unverified recollection.

## 6. Biofeedback/neurofeedback and "AI visualizer"-style tools in cancer care

### Takeaway
Neurofeedback and biofeedback in cancer have only small, low-quality studies, mostly on fatigue, cognition ("chemo brain"), mood and neuropathy symptoms. I found no evidence of any effect on tumour growth, remission or survival. "AI visualizer" tools have no oncology outcome evidence at all. They would sit in the same supportive-care category as guided imagery.

### Cited Findings
- Luctkar-Flude & Groll 2015 (Integr Cancer Ther), a systematic review of neurofeedback for fatigue and cognition in cancer survivors: overall positive findings with few side effects, but methodological quality issues, and "insufficient evidence that neurofeedback is an effective therapy". Only one completed study existed at the time — [SAGE DOI 10.1177/1534735415572886](https://doi.org/10.1177/1534735415572886)
- Hetkamp et al. 2019 (Integr Cancer Ther), a systematic review of neurofeedback in cancer patients — [PMC6416750](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6416750/)
- Fink et al. 2023, an explorative RCT: neurofeedback affected affective symptoms but not perceived cognitive impairment — [PMC9893099](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9893099/)
- A pilot reported that after 20 neurofeedback sessions, breast cancer survivors' self-reported cognition and fatigue returned to population norms (uncontrolled). Another reported long-term reduction in chemotherapy-induced peripheral neuropathy symptoms — [summary of Luctkar-Flude review / pilot literature](https://doi.org/10.1177/1534735415572886); [J Complement Integr Med pilot](https://doi.org/10.1515/jcim-2021-0537)

### Inferences
- An EEG-to-image "AI visualizer" (like the project's `neurovisual/` and TwinOS sensing work) could reasonably be evaluated as a supportive-care or neurofeedback tool, with outcomes such as anxiety, fatigue and QoL in a sham-controlled RCT. There is no basis to claim effects on tumour biology, and doing so would bring it into FDA device-claim territory (section 7).

### Gaps
- I found no studies of AI-generated visualization or VR imagery measuring survival or remission. VR distraction studies for procedural pain or anxiety exist, but I did not retrieve them.

## 7. Regulatory and consumer-protection actions on "frequency" cancer devices (Rife machines etc.)

### Takeaway
No "frequency" or "resonance" device that claims to treat cancer through encoded or tuned signals has FDA approval. The only approved electromagnetic anti-cancer devices are TTFields (section 1) and thermal ablation/hyperthermia systems (section 2). The FDA has issued warning letters about Rife-type devices, and sellers have been criminally prosecuted.

### Cited Findings
- The Rife machine is not FDA-approved for any use. The FDA sent a warning letter to Services Support, Inc. for marketing Rife devices ("Energy Wellness Technology Rife Instrument", "Rife Bio-Energy Systems Technology") without clearance — [Cancer FactFinder](https://cancerfactfinder.org/fact-finder-tool/rife-machines-can-cure-cancer-cancer-fact-finder/); [WebMD](https://www.webmd.com/cancer/cancer-rife-machine-evidence)
- James Folsom: convicted by a jury in February 2009 on 26 felony counts for selling unapproved frequency-generator devices (NatureTronics, AstroPulse, Energy Wellness, etc.). He bought more than 9,000 units, sold them for up to $1,995, and took in more than $8 million in sales. Sentenced in February 2010 to 59 months in prison, a $250,000 fine and 3 years of supervised release. He falsely implied FDA investigational status — [Quackwatch (pointer to DOJ/court records)](https://quackwatch.org/device/reports/rife/folsom/)
- Earlier, three distributors of an energy-medicine device were convicted of device health fraud in 1993–1995 — [Quackwatch](https://quackwatch.org/cancer/reg/life_energy/)
- A review of unproven medical devices in cancer therapy ("big claims but no evidence") is available (I could not fetch the full text because of a captcha) — [PMC3097732](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3097732/)

### Inferences
- Any product or app that claims a DNA-encoded RF signal or AI visualization treats cancer or causes remission would count as a medical device claim under the FD&C Act. It would need FDA clearance or approval backed by clinical evidence, and the FTC requires competent and reliable scientific evidence for health claims. Software can lawfully be described as research, data storage or wellness and stress-management support, as long as it makes no disease-treatment claims.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve primary FDA warning-letter text, DOJ press releases or specific FTC "frequency" or cancer-cure enforcement actions (e.g., FTC Operation Cure.All, 1999–2000) in this session. The Quackwatch items should be checked against the court records they cite.
