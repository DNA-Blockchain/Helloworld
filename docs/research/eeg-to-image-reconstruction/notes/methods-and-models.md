# Methods and models for decoding and reconstructing visual images from scalp EEG (state as of October 2026)

Scope note: non-invasive scalp EEG only; MEG and fMRI appear only as baselines. Numbers below are as reported by each paper (self-reported, mostly arXiv preprints, not independently replicated unless stated). "Within-subject" = intra-subject (train and test on the same person); "cross-subject" = leave-one-subject-out (LOSO). Unless stated otherwise, THINGS-EEG2 retrieval numbers are 200-way zero-shot (200 test concepts never seen in training; chance 0.5% top-1, 2.5% top-5) and are computed on EEG **averaged over the 80 test repetitions** of each test image (see critiques below).

## Main systems: which EEG-to-image retrieval/reconstruction systems exist, their architectures, and their best reported numbers

### Takeaway
The field has split into (a) contrastive EEG-to-CLIP alignment encoders evaluated by 200-way zero-shot retrieval on THINGS-EEG2 (NICE to ATM-S to UBP to 2026 successors), and (b) generators that take those embeddings and condition a pretrained image model (SD 1.5, SDXL + IP-Adapter, DiT, or autoregressive VAR-style models), often in a two-stage "semantic CLIP embedding + low-level blurry/VAE image" pipeline. Older GAN systems and DreamDiffusion/BrainVis were evaluated on the Spampinato EEG-CVPR40 block-design dataset, whose results are confounded and not comparable.

### Cited Findings

**Datasets the systems are evaluated on**
- THINGS-EEG2 (Gifford et al., NeuroImage 264:119754, 2022): 10 subjects, 63 EEG channels at 1000 Hz, RSVP at 5 Hz (100 ms per image), 16,540 training images (1,654 concepts) and 200 test images; >82,000 trials per subject — [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/373510128_A_large_and_rich_EEG_dataset_for_modeling_human_visual_object_recognition); [NEMAR mirror](https://github.com/nemarDatasets/nm000232). Test images are shown 80 times each and the 80 repetitions are averaged at test time in NICE and successors — [NICE, arXiv 2308.13234](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13234).
- EEG-ImageNet (2024): 16 participants, 4,000 ImageNet images in 80 categories, 62 electrodes at 1000 Hz, 0.5 s per image, 87,850 EEG-image pairs; best classification ≈60% (80-class "all" granularity tasks at 80/40/8 classes) and reconstruction 2-way identification ≈64% — [Moonlight review of EEG-ImageNet](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/eeg-imagenet-an-electroencephalogram-dataset-and-benchmarks-with-image-visual-stimuli-of-multi-granularity-labels); [GitHub dataset repo](https://github.com/Promise-Z5Q2SQ/EEG-ImageNet-Dataset).
- Alljoined-1.6M (arXiv 2508.18571, Aug 2025): 20 participants, 4 sessions each (~8 h on task), 16,740 THINGS images, >1.6 M trials, 32-channel Emotiv Flex 2 consumer headset (~$2.2k vs ~$60k research-grade), 256 Hz; training images shown 4–5×, test images 80×; dataset licence CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 — [arXiv 2508.18571](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18571v1). (Earlier: Alljoined1, [arXiv 2404.05553](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.05553).)
- EEG-CVPR40 / "Spampinato" ImageNet-EEG (40 classes) and ThoughtViz: used by Brain2Image, EEGStyleGAN-ADA, DreamDiffusion, BrainVis (see critique in the next section) — [EEGStyleGAN-ADA, arXiv 2310.16532](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16532).

**1. Contrastive EEG–image/CLIP alignment (retrieval)**
- **NICE** (Song et al., ICLR 2024): self-supervised contrastive framework with an EEG encoder (temporal-spatial convolution; NICE-SA adds self-attention, NICE-GA graph attention over electrodes) aligned to a frozen CLIP ViT-L/14 image encoder. THINGS-EEG2 200-way within-subject: NICE 13.8/39.5, NICE-SA 14.7/41.7, NICE-GA 15.6/42.8 (top-1/top-5 %); cross-subject: NICE 6.2/21.4, NICE-SA 7.0/23.1, NICE-GA 5.9/21.6. Input: 63 channels, 0–1000 ms, 250 Hz; 80 test repetitions averaged — [arXiv 2308.13234](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13234).
- **ATM / ATM-S** (Li et al., "Visual Decoding and Reconstruction via EEG Embeddings with Guided Diffusion", NeurIPS 2024): Adaptive Thinking Mapper = channel-wise transformer + temporal-spatial convolution + MLP projector into CLIP space. ATM-S THINGS-EEG2 200-way within-subject 28.64 ± 6.39 / 58.47 ± 8.97; cross-subject 11.84 ± 4.80 / 33.73 ± 8.73 (paper reports NICE 19.32% top-1 as re-run baseline) — [arXiv 2403.07721 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07721). Other papers report ATM-S as 26.1/58.1 ([FSDBN, arXiv 2607.18344](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344)) or 30.15/60.15 ([Alljoined-1.6M](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18571v1)): re-run numbers vary by ±2–4 points.
- **MB2C, CognitionCapturer, Neural-MCRL, VE-SDN** (2024–2025) as tabulated by FSDBN (within-subject, 10-subject mean, top-1/top-5): BraVL 5.8/17.5, NICE 16.1/43.6, MB2C 28.4/60.3, ATM-S 26.1/58.1, CognitionCapturer 33.3/60.6, Neural-MCRL 32.4/64.1, VE-SDN 37.2/69.9 — [arXiv 2607.18344](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344).
- **UBP, Uncertainty-aware Blur Prior** (Wu et al., arXiv 2503.04207, March 2025): blurs the *image* side with a Gaussian whose strength is adapted per sample by an uncertainty estimate, to model the "system gap" (foveal/peripheral information loss) and "random gap" (attention, noise). Brain encoder "EEGProject" is only two linear layers with residuals (5.4 M params); CLIP RN50/ViT image encoders; 17 occipital/parietal channels, 250 Hz, 0–1000 ms. THINGS-EEG2 within-subject 50.9/79.7 (vs. 37.2/69.9 prior); THINGS-MEG within-subject 26.7/55.2 — [arXiv 2503.04207](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04207). Cross-subject 12.4/33.4 per FSDBN's table — [arXiv 2607.18344](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344).
- **FSDBN** (arXiv 2607.18344, July 2026): foreground-aware alignment using SAM (ViT-H) masks, CLIP ViT-B/16, BLIP-2 captions; EEG encoder = 3-layer GAT (8 heads) + 3-layer TCN, dim 256. THINGS-EEG2 within-subject 69.0/92.2; cross-subject 15.0/37.1; THINGS-MEG within-subject 34.7/69.4 — [arXiv 2607.18344](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344).
- **MindAlign** (Chen et al., KTH/Cambridge/Karolinska/EPFL, arXiv 2605.24523, May 2026): EEG–vision–language alignment; encoder = subject-specific adaptation layer + graph attention over channels + transformer + channel-wise attention with spatial priors + temporal-spatial conv patch embedding; best image/text encoder was CN-CLIP ResNet-50 (38 M params), beating ViT-L/H/G-14. THINGS-EEG2 within-subject 54.1/83.4 (vs. its strongest listed baseline 32.4/64.0); cross-subject 7.8–23.8% top-1 and 29.5–51.5% top-5 across subjects — [arXiv 2605.24523](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24523). Note: its stated "strongest prior baseline" (32.4/64.0 = Neural-MCRL) is below UBP's published 50.9/79.7; the fetched summary was inconsistent about whether UBP is in its table.
- **UMind** (arXiv 2509.14772): unified multitask M/EEG decoder; reported 54.8/85.6 within-subject on THINGS-EEG — [search snippet of arXiv 2509.14772](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14772) (not fetched in full).
- **MB2L** (Liu et al., NUAA, arXiv 2605.04680, May 2026): adaptive retinotopic Gaussian blur (ABVP), biomimetic multi-level visual features from a ResNet-50 image encoder with channel-attention priors, multi-level bidirectional contrastive loss. THINGS-EEG2 within-subject **80.5/97.6**, cross-subject **19.8/44.7**; 80 test trials averaged — [arXiv 2605.04680](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04680).
- **HCF + NEAR** (Cui, Kan, Liu, Wu, arXiv 2608.19128, Aug 2026): with the HCF base retriever, 200-way top-1 is 14.0% with 1 test repetition, 43.2% with 4, 83.4% with 80; NEAR (denoiser pulling noisy query toward the high-repetition "anchor" + image-side pseudo-anchor predictor) raises these to 19.8%, 52.5% and 87.6% — [arXiv 2608.19128](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19128). The base HCF figure was not verified against its own paper (likely [arXiv 2602.07495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07495), whose abstract does not state numbers).
- **AVDE** encoder (see below): fine-tuned LaBraM foundation model, THINGS-EEG2 within-subject 30.0/58.2, cross-subject 14.3/32.9 — [arXiv 2602.22555](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22555).

**2. EEG encoders feeding diffusion models (latent diffusion, SDXL, IP-Adapter) and two-stage semantic + low-level pipelines**
- **ATM-S reconstruction** (NeurIPS 2024): two-stage. Stage I: EEG embedding → CLIP image prior (diffusion prior) and a lightweight U-Net/VAE path for a low-level blurry image; Stage II: SDXL + IP-Adapter conditioned on the predicted CLIP embedding, the low-level image (img2img) and a generated caption. Subject 8 metrics, EEG / MEG / fMRI: PixCorr 0.160 / 0.104 / 0.305; SSIM 0.345 / 0.340 / 0.366; AlexNet(2) 0.776 / 0.613 / 0.962; CLIP 0.786 / 0.603 / 0.917 — [arXiv 2403.07721](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07721).
- **CognitionCapturer** (Zhang et al., arXiv 2412.10489, Dec 2024): "Modality Expert Encoders" align EEG separately to image, text and depth representations, a diffusion prior maps EEG embeddings into CLIP space, and a frozen pretrained generator (no fine-tuning) reconstructs — [arXiv 2412.10489](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.10489). Retrieval 33.3/60.6 per [FSDBN table](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344); reconstruction (subject 8) PixCorr 0.175, SSIM 0.366, CLIP 0.744, SwAV 0.577 per [AVDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22555).
- **DreamDiffusion** (Bai et al., arXiv 2306.16934, 2023; older result): MAE-style temporal masked-signal pretraining of the EEG encoder on ~120,000 EEG samples from >400 subjects (MOABB; 30–128 channels; motor imagery, object viewing, video; 75% mask ratio, 4 time steps per token, 1024-d embeddings), then fine-tuning Stable Diffusion 1.5 conditioned on EEG, with CLIP image-embedding supervision. EEG band-passed 5–95 Hz, 512 samples. Evaluated on EEG-CVPR40 by "50-way top-1 accuracy" of a pretrained ImageNet classifier on generated images: 45.8% full model; 28.3% without CLIP supervision; 4.2% without pretraining. Encoder size 74–458 M params tested. Authors state EEG "only provide[s] coarse-grained information at the category level" — [arXiv 2306.16934 (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.16934).
- **BrainVis** (Fu et al., arXiv 2312.14871; revised Sep 2024): self-supervised time-domain masked pretraining plus frequency-domain features, aligned to an interpolation of coarse (class label) and fine-grained CLIP semantics, then cascaded diffusion; claims better semantic fidelity and generation quality than prior work using "only 10% training data of the previous work" (EEG-CVPR40) — [arXiv 2312.14871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14871).
- **Perceptogram** (OpenReview): reconstructions from EEG with image generative models using a linear mapping — [OpenReview IZOeRDS6zU](https://openreview.net/forum?id=IZOeRDS6zU).
- **ENIGMA** (Alljoined): multi-subject EEG-to-image model; THINGS-EEG2 retrieval 27.60/59.35/71.15 (top-1/5/10), on Alljoined-1.6M 6.00/16.25/25.35 (ATM-S on Alljoined-1.6M only 0.50/2.00/5.00). Reconstruction on Alljoined-1.6M: ENIGMA PixCorr 0.079, SSIM 0.416, CLIP 62.91%, human 2AFC identification 65.43% (545 raters); ATM-S 0.090 / 0.374 / 56.25% / 60.31%; Perceptogram 0.094 / 0.401 / 59.94% / 62.00% — [arXiv 2508.18571](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18571v1).
- **JMVR** (Beijing Univ. of Technology, arXiv 2603.19667, Mar 2026): joint EEG + text conditioning of a Diffusion Transformer. THINGS-EEG reconstruction table (PixCorr / SSIM / Alex(2) / Alex(5) / Inception / CLIP / SwAV↓): NICE 0.173/0.326/0.783/0.832/0.746/0.773/0.523; MUSE 0.199/0.358/0.802/0.853/0.754/0.793/0.513; ATM 0.182/0.353/0.776/0.866/0.755/0.790/0.545; DreamDiffusion 0.206/0.347/0.787/0.857/0.751/0.791/0.504; Perceptogram 0.214/0.334/0.856/0.874/0.762/0.818/0.531; CognitionCapturer 0.178/0.359/0.806/0.894/0.769/0.803/0.552; **JMVR 0.236/0.372/0.874/0.920/0.793/0.829/0.458**. Trained on 2× A40 48 GB — [arXiv 2603.19667](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19667). Note the joint text input: JMVR is not EEG-only.
- **AVDE** (Dai, Xiao, Yu, Ye; CASIA/BAAI, arXiv 2602.22555, Feb–Mar 2026): LaBraM foundation encoder (pretrained on 2000+ h EEG) fine-tuned contrastively to CLIP, then a VAR-style autoregressive "next-scale prediction" generator over a multi-scale VQ-VAE (coarse-to-fine). Subject 8: PixCorr 0.188, SSIM 0.396, CLIP 0.795, SwAV 0.557 (vs. ATM 0.160/0.345/0.786/0.582). Also evaluated on EEG-ImageNet and THINGS-MEG — [arXiv 2602.22555](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22555).
- Other 2025–2026 reconstruction preprints found but not fetched: saliency-guided diffusion ([arXiv 2510.26391](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.26391)), structure-guided diffusion ([arXiv 2604.22649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.22649)), Uni-Neur2Img DiT ([arXiv 2512.18635](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18635)), EEG2Vision ([arXiv 2604.08063](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08063)), Brain3D EEG-to-3D ([arXiv 2604.08068](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08068)), GAN-based brain-to-image ([arXiv 2609.22282](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22282)), EEG-to-video critique "Beyond Reconstruction" ([arXiv 2505.21385](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.21385)).

**3. Generative and adversarial approaches (mostly 2017–2024, EEG-CVPR40/ThoughtViz)**
- Brain2Image-VAE / Brain2Image-GAN (Kavasidis et al. 2017): IS 5.07 on EEG-CVPR40 for the GAN — as cited in [EEGStyleGAN-ADA, arXiv 2310.16532](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16532).
- **EEGStyleGAN-ADA** (Singh et al., WACV 2024): LSTM EEG encoder trained with triplet loss, features concatenated with noise into StyleGAN2-ADA. IS 10.82 / FID 174.13 on EEG-CVPR40; IS 9.23 / FID 109.49 on ThoughtViz (+62.9% and +36.13% IS over prior SOTA) — [arXiv 2310.16532](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.16532); code [prajwalsingh/EEGStyleGAN-ADA](https://github.com/prajwalsingh/EEGStyleGAN-ADA). Predecessor EEG2IMAGE (ICASSP 2023) — [arXiv 2302.10121](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.10121).
- A 2025 review (IEEE TBME) organises EEG reconstruction decoders into GAN-, diffusion- and autoencoder-based, and encoders into CNN, LSTM, Transformer and multimodal types — [ResearchGate: "Deep Learning for EEG-Based Visual Classification and Reconstruction: Panorama, Trends, Challenges and Opportunities"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391603071_Deep_Learning_for_EEG-Based_Visual_Classification_and_Reconstruction_Panorama_Trends_Challenges_and_Opportunities); [DOI 10.1109/tbme.2025.3568282](https://doi.org/10.1109/tbme.2025.3568282). A broader survey of diffusion models in neuroimaging: [arXiv 2502.06552](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.06552).

**4. MindEye-style adaptation to EEG**
- No dedicated "MindEye for EEG" paper was found; the ATM-S two-stage design (diffusion prior to CLIP space + low-level img2img + SDXL/IP-Adapter) is the de-facto EEG analogue, and the same group reports EEG, MEG and fMRI with one pipeline — [arXiv 2403.07721](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07721).

### Inferences
- Since 2024 the benchmark has converged on THINGS-EEG2 200-way zero-shot retrieval and on the NSD-derived reconstruction metric set (PixCorr, SSIM, AlexNet(2/5), Inception, CLIP, SwAV/EffNet). EEG-CVPR40-era numbers (IS/FID, "50-way classifier accuracy") are not comparable with them.
- Much of the 2025–2026 progress comes from changes on the image side (blurring, foreground masks, choice of CLIP backbone, multiple visual encoders) rather than from larger EEG encoders. UBP's encoder is two linear layers, and MindAlign's best image encoder was a small ResNet-50 CLIP, not ViT-G. This points to an alignment-target problem: EEG carries coarse, low-spatial-frequency, category-level information.
- EEG reconstruction metrics cluster tightly (CLIP 0.74–0.83, SSIM 0.33–0.40, PixCorr 0.16–0.24 on THINGS-EEG), well below fMRI (MindEye2 CLIP 0.93, PixCorr 0.32). The generator's prior supplies most of the image detail.

### Gaps
- UBP's venue (widely cited as CVPR 2025) was not confirmed from a primary page; the arXiv version is cited.
- BrainVis's exact numbers (IS, FID, classification accuracy), CognitionCapturer's full retrieval and reconstruction tables from its own paper, MB2C, Neural-MCRL, VE-SDN, NECOMIMI, ViEEG, BrainFLORA, NeuroBridge, UMind and "EEGVis" were not fetched directly. Their numbers come from other papers' tables or from search snippets, and "EEGVis" could not be identified as a distinct published system.
- The HCF base retriever's 83.4% top-1 (80 repetitions) was reported only by the NEAR paper and is unverified. It coincides numerically with MindAlign's top-5 (83.4%), so it should be checked before use.

## State of the art on THINGS-EEG2 200-way zero-shot retrieval and comparison with MEG and fMRI

### Takeaway
As of October 2026 the highest self-reported within-subject THINGS-EEG2 200-way result is MB2L's 80.5% top-1 / 97.6% top-5 (arXiv, May 2026, not peer-reviewed), with HCF+NEAR claiming 87.6% top-1 and FSDBN 69.0/92.2. The last widely cited peer-reviewed-era result is UBP's 50.9/79.7 (2025). Cross-subject accuracy remains low at 15–20% top-1. All of these use EEG averaged over 80 repetitions. Single-trial top-1 is about 14–20%, roughly the level of 2023 averaged-trial NICE. fMRI (MindEye2 on NSD, 98.8% top-1 in 300-way) remains far ahead. MEG on THINGS-MEG is now below EEG on the same retrieval protocol, likely because THINGS-MEG has only 4 subjects and fewer repetitions.

### Cited Findings
- Progression of within-subject THINGS-EEG2 200-way top-1/top-5: NICE-GA 15.6/42.8 (ICLR 2024) → ATM-S 28.6/58.5 (NeurIPS 2024) → VE-SDN 37.2/69.9 → UBP 50.9/79.7 (Mar 2025) → MindAlign 54.1/83.4 (May 2026) → FSDBN 69.0/92.2 (Jul 2026) → MB2L 80.5/97.6 (May 2026) — [NICE](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13234); [ATM](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07721); [UBP](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04207); [MindAlign](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24523); [FSDBN](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344); [MB2L](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04680).
- Cross-subject (LOSO) progression: NICE 6.2/21.4 → ATM-S 11.8/33.7 → UBP 12.4/33.4 → AVDE 14.3/32.9 → FSDBN 15.0/37.1 → MB2L 19.8/44.7 — sources as above plus [AVDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22555). MindAlign authors: "cross-subject Top-1 accuracy (~15%) remains well below the in-subject ceiling" — [arXiv 2605.24523](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24523).
- Trial averaging: 200-way top-1 falls from 83.4% (80 repetitions averaged) to 43.2% (4) to 14.0% (1 repetition); NEAR raises single-trial top-1 to 19.8% — [arXiv 2608.19128](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19128). FSDBN and MB2L explicitly average repeated test trials for SNR — [FSDBN](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344); [MB2L](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04680).
- **MEG baseline (Meta, Benchetrit et al., "Brain decoding: toward real-time reconstruction of visual perception", arXiv 2310.19812, Oct 2023; older result):** THINGS-MEG (4 subjects, 272 gradiometers, 22,448 unique images), retrieval top-5 ≈70% on the small test set (200 images; VGG-19 70.33%, CLIP-Vision 68.66%, DINOv2 68.00%) and ≈13% on the large set (2,400 images); a 7× improvement over linear decoders. Reconstruction MEG vs. NSD fMRI: PixCorr 0.076 vs 0.305, SSIM 0.336 vs 0.366, AlexNet(5) 0.826 vs 0.977, CLIP 0.767 vs 0.917, SwAV 0.584 vs 0.410. Decoding peaks 200–275 ms after onset (100 ms sliding windows, 25 ms stride) — [arXiv 2310.19812](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19812).
- MEG with EEG-derived methods: UBP 26.7/55.2 and FSDBN 34.7/69.4 within-subject 200-way on THINGS-MEG, both well below the same methods on EEG — [UBP](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04207); [FSDBN](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344).
- **fMRI baseline (MindEye2, Scotti et al., ICML 2024):** NSD 7T, 40 h per subject: 300-way top-1 image retrieval 98.8%, brain retrieval 98.3%, PixCorr 0.322, SSIM 0.431, AlexNet(2) 96.1%, AlexNet(5) 98.6%, Inception 95.4%, CLIP 93.0%. With 1 h of subject data: 79.0% / 57.4% retrieval, PixCorr 0.195, SSIM 0.419, CLIP 79.2% — [arXiv 2403.11207](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11207).
- Cross-modality scaling (Banville, Benchetrit, d'Ascoli, Rapin, King, Meta, arXiv 2501.15322, Jan 2025): 8 datasets, 84 subjects, 498 h (EEG 66 subjects incl. THINGS-EEG1/2). 7T fMRI gives the best decoding "across the board". Performance is log-linear in data with no saturation. More within-subject data helps steadily, whereas adding subjects gives "modest, if any" gains. Deep learning gains over linear baselines are largest for noisy modalities (EEG 1.9–2.4×, 7T fMRI 1.2×). Estimated acquisition cost per hour: EEG $263, MEG $550, 3T fMRI $935, 7T fMRI $1,093 — [arXiv 2501.15322](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15322).
- **Methodological critiques:**
  - Block-design confound in Spampinato EEG-CVPR40: Li, Johansen, Ahmed, Ilyevsky, Wilbur, Bharadwaj and Siskind (IEEE TPAMI 43(1):316–333, 2020) showed the high accuracies depended on a block design in which every test trial came from a block with many trials in the training set, so classifiers learned slow block-level brain state rather than stimulus perception. With a rapid-event design the results did not replicate, and a random codebook did as well as EEG-derived representations — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-Perils-and-Pitfalls-of-Block-Design-for-EEG-Li-Johansen/20cdd09586561255c261902c11315df4a8a4d11c); [DOI 10.1109/tpami.2020.2973153](https://doi.org/10.1109/tpami.2020.2973153). Spampinato's group published a rebuttal, "Correct block-design experiments mitigate temporal correlation bias in EEG classification" — [bioRxiv 2020.12.05.403402](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2020.12.05.403402v1.full). DreamDiffusion, BrainVis, EEGStyleGAN-ADA and Brain2Image all report on this dataset.
  - Repeated-stimulus confound (Kilgallen, Pearlmutter, Siskind, arXiv 2508.00531, Aug 2025): training and testing on responses to the same repeated stimuli lets stimulus identity leak. 16 publications were affected, with accuracy overestimated by 4.46–7.42 points (+0.26 points of overestimation per 1 point of confounded accuracy) — [arXiv 2508.00531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.00531). Which specific papers or datasets are affected was not visible in the abstract.
  - Averaging over 80 repetitions means headline numbers "should not be interpreted as real-time or single-trial decoding" — [arXiv 2608.19128](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19128).
  - Consumer hardware: on Alljoined-1.6M (32-channel Emotiv), ATM-S collapses to 0.5% top-1 and ENIGMA reaches 6.0% top-1 — [arXiv 2508.18571](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18571v1).
  - MB2L limitation: generic temporal modelling; assumes a fixed central fixation and ignores microsaccades — [arXiv 2605.04680](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04680). JMVR: current metrics emphasise depth and colour and lack assessment of higher-level cognition — [arXiv 2603.19667](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19667).

### Inferences
- The 2026 jumps (50.9 → 69–80.5% top-1) are preprint-only, use different image encoders (ResNet-50, CN-CLIP, SAM+CLIP+BLIP-2) and test-time averaging, and have not been independently reproduced. The report should present UBP (50.9/79.7) as the established reference point and MB2L/FSDBN as claimed 2026 SOTA.
- The N differs across modalities (EEG 200-way, MindEye2 300-way, MEG small set 200 images reported as top-5), so the comparison is indicative only. Under any reading, fMRI retrieval is near ceiling while single-trial EEG is still ~14–20% top-1.
- Because EEG-to-CLIP retrieval works across 200 unseen concepts while per-trial accuracy is low, current EEG decoding is better described as category- and coarse-feature-level, which matches DreamDiffusion's own stated limitation.

### Gaps
- No independent leaderboard for THINGS-EEG2 (Papers with Code or similar) was found; the SOTA progression above is assembled from each paper's own comparison tables.
- Which papers fall under the repeated-stimulus confound (arXiv 2508.00531) was not determined.
- No independent replication of the MB2L, FSDBN or MindAlign numbers was found.

## What carries the signal: channels, time window, frequency bands, subject-specific training, foundation-model pretraining

### Takeaway
Most decodable visual information sits in occipital (then parietal/temporal) electrodes, roughly 100–600 ms after onset with peaks near 200–400 ms, and in theta and beta bands rather than gamma. Subject-specific training and more within-subject data matter far more than adding subjects. EEG foundation-model pretraining (LaBraM, MOABB-style MAE) helps modestly. AVDE's LaBraM encoder reaches only ATM-S-level retrieval (30.0% top-1), far below image-side tricks such as blur priors.

### Cited Findings
- Time window: NICE found the region of interest at 100–600 ms within its 0–1000 ms input — [arXiv 2308.13234](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13234). ATM: peak accuracy in the 200–400 ms range, with a plateau using <500 ms of EEG — [arXiv 2403.07721](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07721). MEG decoding peaks 200–275 ms after onset — [arXiv 2310.19812](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19812). Alljoined-1.6M pairwise LDA decoding peaks at 100, 220 and 400 ms — [arXiv 2508.18571](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18571v1). UBP uses 0–1000 ms at 250 Hz — [arXiv 2503.04207](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04207).
- Channels and regions: removing occipital electrodes costs NICE 3.8 points of accuracy; temporal and parietal electrodes also contribute — [arXiv 2308.13234](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13234). ATM: "Occipital had the highest retrieval accuracy and reconstruction performance", parietal moderate — [arXiv 2403.07721](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07721). UBP's 50.9% uses only 17 occipital/parietal channels — [arXiv 2503.04207](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04207). Alljoined-1.6M: diminishing returns beyond 24 channels — [arXiv 2508.18571](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18571v1).
- Frequency: NICE found theta (~4 Hz) and beta (~14–18 Hz) effective and gamma minimal — [arXiv 2308.13234](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13234). DreamDiffusion band-passes 5–95 Hz — [ar5iv 2306.16934](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.16934).
- Subject-specific vs. cross-subject: every method loses roughly 2–4× top-1 going cross-subject (e.g. MB2L 80.5 → 19.8, FSDBN 69.0 → 15.0, ATM-S 28.6 → 11.8) — [MB2L](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04680); [FSDBN](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344); [ATM](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07721). Within-subject data scales log-linearly, while across-subject data adds "modest, if any" gains — [arXiv 2501.15322](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.15322). Alljoined-1.6M accuracy rises almost linearly with log trial count with no saturation at 1.6 M trials — [arXiv 2508.18571](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18571v1).
- Foundation-model pretraining: DreamDiffusion's MAE pretraining on ~120k MOABB samples is critical within its pipeline (4.2% without vs 45.8% with, EEG-CVPR40 50-way generated-image accuracy) — [ar5iv 2306.16934](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.16934). AVDE fine-tunes LaBraM (pretrained on 2000+ h of EEG) and reaches 30.0/58.2 within-subject and 14.3/32.9 cross-subject — [arXiv 2602.22555](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22555). LaBraM was trained mainly on clinical rather than visual-stimulus EEG, and is adapted to visual decoding through contrastive fine-tuning — [Emergent Mind LaBraM summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/labram). BIOT: no visual-decoding result was found.
- Image-side priors carry much of the gain: degrading high-frequency image detail (Gaussian blur) "significantly enhance[s] retrieval performance" and is not merely data augmentation — [arXiv 2503.04207](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04207). MB2L uses retinotopic adaptive blur — [arXiv 2605.04680](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04680). FSDBN uses foreground saliency — [arXiv 2607.18344](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344).
- Hardware SNR: on the same THINGS images and protocol, the 32-channel consumer headset gives 6.0% top-1 (ENIGMA) vs 27.6% on research-grade THINGS-EEG2 — [arXiv 2508.18571](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.18571v1).

### Inferences
- The evidence favours a compact montage of about 17–24 occipital/parietal channels, a 0–500/600 ms post-onset window, and low/mid frequency content. That suits low-cost hardware but needs many within-subject trials.
- Foundation EEG models have not yet shown a clear advantage on THINGS-EEG2. The largest gains come from what the EEG is aligned to (the image representation) and from trial averaging.

### Gaps
- No controlled head-to-head of LaBraM, BIOT or other EEG foundation models against from-scratch encoders on THINGS-EEG2 was found. EEG-FM-Audit ([arXiv 2605.26910](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.26910)) and EEG-Xplain ([arXiv 2609.15687](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.15687)) may contain such data but were not fetched.
- Frequency-band ablations beyond NICE (e.g., in UBP or MB2L) were not found.

## Compute: training requirements and real-time feasibility

### Takeaway
Contrastive EEG encoders are cheap: minutes per subject on one RTX 4090/3090, or a few V100s. Reconstruction adds a frozen multi-billion-parameter diffusion stack (ATM ≈3.8 B params, ~310 ms inference), while autoregressive generators cut this to ~91 ms. Inference latency is compatible with near-real-time use. The accuracy is not, because the headline figures depend on averaging 80 repetitions and single-trial top-1 is ~14–20%.

### Cited Findings
- NICE: ~5 minutes per subject on one GeForce RTX 4090, up to 200 epochs, batch 1000 — [arXiv 2308.13234](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.13234).
- ATM: single NVIDIA RTX 4090 — [arXiv 2403.07721](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07721).
- UBP: four V100 GPUs, 50 epochs, batch 1024 — [arXiv 2503.04207](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.04207).
- MindAlign: single RTX 4090, ~5 min per subject (stage 1) + ~3 min (stage 2), AdamW lr 2e-4, batch 1000 (EEG) / 500 (MEG) — [arXiv 2605.24523](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.24523).
- MB2L: RTX 3090 24 GB, 60 epochs, batch 256; training time not reported — [arXiv 2605.04680](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04680).
- FSDBN: 100 epochs, Adam 1e-4 (GPU not captured) — [arXiv 2607.18344](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.18344).
- JMVR (DiT reconstruction): 2× NVIDIA A40 48 GB — [arXiv 2603.19667](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.19667).
- DreamDiffusion: 500 epochs of pretraining + 300 epochs of fine-tuning; hardware not reported — [ar5iv 2306.16934](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2306.16934).
- Inference efficiency (AVDE vs. Li et al. 2024 ATM diffusion pipeline): 425.3 M vs 3,818.1 M params; 1,350.5 vs 8,738.6 GFLOPs; 91.2 vs 310.4 ms per image; 1.81 vs 4.83 GB memory — [arXiv 2602.22555](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22555).
- Comparison baselines: Meta MEG decoder trained on a single 32 GB Volta GPU — [arXiv 2310.19812](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19812). MindEye2 single-subject models trained on one 8×A100 80 GB node for 150 epochs — [arXiv 2403.11207](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.11207).
- Real-time caveat: averaging 80 test repetitions means results are not real-time or single-trial decoding; single-repetition top-1 is 14.0% (19.8% with NEAR) — [arXiv 2608.19128](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19128). Meta's MEG work framed the goal as "toward real-time reconstruction", using 100 ms sliding windows — [arXiv 2310.19812](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19812).

### Inferences
- A per-subject contrastive encoder can be trained on a single consumer GPU in under 10 minutes. The data, not the compute, is the bottleneck: THINGS-EEG2 has >82k trials per subject from long lab sessions.
- With AVDE-class generators (~91 ms) plus a millisecond-scale encoder, end-to-end generation at about 5–10 Hz is technically feasible. Each output would come from a single trial, with accuracy near 14–20% top-1 for 200-way retrieval.

### Gaps
- GPU-hours for the larger reconstruction pipelines (ATM Stage I/II training, CognitionCapturer, AVDE fine-tuning) were not reported in the fetched pages.
- No published closed-loop, online EEG-to-image system with measured end-to-end latency and online accuracy was found.

## Code: open-source repositories, weights and licences

### Takeaway
Most major EEG systems have public PyTorch code under MIT, and UBP uses Apache-2.0. CognitionCapturer's arXiv listing says CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 while its GitHub repo reports MIT. AVDE's repo declares no licence. Pretrained EEG-decoder weights on Hugging Face were not confirmed in this session. Datasets carry their own (often non-commercial) licences.

### Inferences
- MIT-licensed code exists for NICE, ATM-S, DreamDiffusion, BrainVis, EEGStyleGAN-ADA, ENIGMA, LaBraM and BIOT. A project can reuse it, provided that the generators it depends on (SDXL, IP-Adapter, Stable Diffusion 1.5) are used under their own model licences and the datasets under theirs. Alljoined-1.6M's licence is non-commercial with no derivatives.
- AVDE (no licence) cannot legally be reused beyond viewing until its authors add one. CognitionCapturer's licence conflict should be resolved by checking the repo's LICENSE file before reuse.
